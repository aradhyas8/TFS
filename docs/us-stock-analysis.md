# US stock analysis in the shared decision pipeline

Select a USD stock listing on XNAS, XNYS or XASE and ask the question through the existing form. A candidate can have zero shares. `POST /api/analyze` adds optional `stock: {position_id}`. Python binds the selected issuer, snapshot and sources; the model cannot submit financial facts, replace holdings or waive settings. Existing portfolio-only requests retain their behavior.

A stock request requires `review_portfolio`, `get_sec_filings`, `get_issuer_material`, `calculate_company_cases` and `calculate_comparison` before its answer. The shared loop is bounded to twelve turns for stock requests and eight otherwise. Stock calculations must precede comparison. Without an explicit comparison, the backend selects company, first available diversified ETF, first nonzero cash row and no action. The scope is the actual selected stock position, or available cash for a zero-share candidate. These are bounded alternatives, not a discovery scan. A custom comparison can include up to four alternatives and select a different actual capital scope. Relevant existing holdings remain visible across accounts; unresearched retained companies remain unknown.

## Primary evidence configuration

Set backend-only `RESEARCH_REFERENCE_FILE` to an independently reviewed JSON file mapping company IDs to `CompanyResearch` records. FastAPI `/docs` and `backend/analyst/schemas.py` define the fields. `get_sec_filings` reads dated original SEC filing extracts; `get_issuer_material` reads issuer investor-relations extracts from this request-local record. This adapter does **not** fetch live documents, discover filings, parse arbitrary uploads or claim automatic XBRL verification. The operator must update reviewed extracts for the decision date. No reference means evidence remains unavailable and the completed recommendation requests the decision-critical inputs.

Each document has ID, issuer, original HTTPS URL, authority (`sec`, `issuer`, or thesis-specific `macro`), publication date, financial as-of date, title, reviewed excerpt, availability and an explicit Q&A-availability flag. SEC URLs must point to the original `/Archives/edgar/data/` document on `www.sec.gov`. Preserve issuer publication URLs, not a convenience parser as authority. Future-dated or empty extracts are unavailable. Any future network adapter must follow [SEC developer and fair-access guidance](https://www.sec.gov/about/developer-resources) and identify its user agent; this adapter makes no network requests.

Facts preserve the metric, unscaled currency/shares unit, currency, period start/end, definition, source document IDs and independent checks of the filing, material notes, custom tags and segments. An unavailable original filing, incompatible units/currency, nonannual operating period, incompatible share period, unchecked notes/tags/segments or contradictory same-period records leaves the calculation unknown. Records are shown as reported inputs with their checks; they are not silently promoted to verified facts. Revenue/FFO inputs must cover an annual period. All amounts are unscaled; never mix thousands or millions without converting and disclosing the definition. This initial adapter supports revenue, diluted shares, total book value and FFO, rather than attempting a universal financial statement parser.

## Company judgments and Python calculations

`calculate_company_cases` accepts referenced fact IDs, method, mid-cycle context and exactly one downside/base/upside path. Each case exposes five annual growth, net margin, cash conversion, reinvestment, dilution, payout and FX assumptions, plus discount rate, exit multiple, three exit sensitivities, assumptions and uncertainty. These are **judgments**, not reported facts. Cyclical or unknown cyclicality requires mid-cycle context.

- Operating companies: annual revenue times net margin produces net earnings. `earnings_exit` applies an earnings multiple; cash conversion must be neutral and reinvestment zero because net earnings are not FCF.
- `fcf_exit`: net earnings times cash conversion times `(1 - reinvestment)` produces modeled FCF. Conversion describes cash before the separately modeled reinvestment; define this carefully to avoid deducting reinvestment twice.
- Financial companies: `book_exit` grows total reported book value directly and applies a price/book exit multiple. Growth is net of payout effects. An explicit annual return-on-equity path times opening book produces earnings for the payout calculation; book capital itself is not dividend income. Missing ROE with a positive payout leaves the case unknown. Capital adequacy remains source-backed judgment. No industrial revenue-margin shortcut is used.
- REITs: `ffo_exit` grows reported FFO directly and applies a price/FFO exit multiple. The cited FFO definition and reinvestment/capital needs must be reviewed. The current case does not claim FFO equals distributable cash.

Book/FFO use neutral margin and cash-conversion paths and zero reinvestment transforms. Diluted shares compound explicitly. Exit price is the nonnegative terminal metric times multiple divided by terminal diluted shares. Loss-making cases floor equity exit at zero in this multiple method and pay no negative dividend; they do not assume a positive recovery value. Payout uses modeled per-share earnings, FCF or FFO; book-value payout uses explicit ROE earnings on opening book rather than book capital; distributions convert once with that year's FX and remain idle reporting cash. Present value discounts local distributions and the terminal price. Exit sensitivities vary the multiple with operating assumptions held fixed. Reverse valuation solves the year-5 exit multiple at which the discounted exit price plus discounted payouts equals the dated quote under that operating path; it does not uniquely infer market beliefs. Five years is a comparison horizon, not a sell date. There are no probabilities, weighted values, inflation claims or return hurdles.

`calculate_comparison` scales the company per-share reporting outcome to the same actual selected capital as ETFs and cash. No action preserves each actual scope row and uses its researched stock case where available. Stock drivers cannot supply ETF-style return paths, inconsistent FX assumptions or reinvestment; company distributions remain idle cash. Known effects follow ticket 04; unknown transaction and tax consequences stay unquantified. Company position outcomes are before those unknown effects and are distinguished from common-capital comparisons.

## Recommendation and limitations

Stock actions can be conditional add, hold, reduce, exit or no action, with one or two alternatives. Material claims cite `evidence_ids` that link to dated primary documents. The reason explains strengths and portfolio fit, downside names failure mechanisms, assumptions distinguish judgments, and uncertainty and change triggers stay explicit. Unsupported numeric prose, probabilities, allocations, execution claims, unavailable evidence or claimed unavailable Q&A fail validation. Existing deterministic cap/budget checks can replace every final answer field, preventing hidden waivers. Missing primary facts produce clarification. Conditional adds require usable sources, applicable numeric limits and a passing user preview; no automatic order exists.

`amount` remains null in this implementation. A scenario outcome, an exposure gap or a passing hypothetical preview does not establish justified sizing. Cost basis does not anchor the thesis; no contribution room or personal tax effects are inferred from account labels. The deployment still needs a configured OpenAI model, financial identity/quote/FX sources and reviewed company records to produce substantive research. Codex only builds the app and tests never contact live model or market services.

## Verification and separate answer review

API tests use a fictional issuer, dated portfolio, primary-document extracts, model tool scripts and real Python calculations. Browser tests exercise the selector, same-request orchestration, dated links, company cases and cash alternatives. The SDK boundary uses fake HTTP transport. Socket guards prohibit live services.

The fixture answer is reviewed for internal consistency: its conditional hold acknowledges concentration; primary source records support only the fictional revenue/share inputs, company cases distinguish margin outcomes and cash alternatives, unavailable Q&A is not claimed, and unknown costs/taxes/sizing are disclosed. This is a **fixed-answer contract review**, not evidence of live model research or investment-judgment quality. Assess completed live application answers separately for primary-claim support, issuer-specific ratios and notes, mid-cycle normalization, reinvestment/dilution consistency, thesis failures, alternative relevance, guardrail fit and decision-changing uncertainty. No live quality assessment is claimed here.
