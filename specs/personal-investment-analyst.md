## Problem Statement

The investor needs help deciding what to do with new cash, reviewing a whole portfolio, evaluating a stock, or exploring a theme. These decisions depend on current holdings, price, evidence, alternatives, concentration, and uncertainty. A company that looks attractive on its own may still be the wrong portfolio choice at today's price or exposure.

The user needs one simple, on-demand decision process that compares stocks with diversified ETFs, cash, and no action. It must show what is known, what is calculated, and what remains a judgment. It must not imply certainty or invent a confident allocation when key facts or guardrails are missing.

## Solution

Provide one analyst experience for four user requests: allocate new cash, review or rebalance current holdings, assess a named stock, and explore a theme. The deployed application is a standalone full-stack app: a Next.js frontend accepts portfolio input and investment questions, and a Python/FastAPI backend uses the OpenAI API for reasoning and tool orchestration, invokes financial/research tools and deterministic Python calculations, and returns a structured recommendation for the frontend to display. One investment question returns a completed recommendation in that same request. Each answer gives a preferred action, an approximate amount when the evidence and portfolio inputs justify one, the main reason, one or two alternatives, downside, assumptions, uncertainty, and the facts that could change the conclusion.

Use one dated, whole-portfolio view and compare serious alternatives on a common reporting-currency and date basis. Present conditional downside, base, and upside cases over five years using methods suited to each instrument. Keep calculations transparent and use cited, dated evidence for material claims. Request only missing information that could materially change the decision; when it is unavailable, complete the response with conditional direction or explain that the evidence cannot distinguish the options.

## User Stories

1. As an investor deciding what to do with new cash, I want a preferred action and an approximate allocation when justified, so that I can put the money to work in line with my whole portfolio.
2. As an investor allocating new cash, I want the analyst to confirm the amount, account, and only decision-critical context, so that the recommendation reflects my situation without a lengthy questionnaire.
3. As an investor allocating new cash, I want a lightweight fresh scan on every request, so that the analyst considers current opportunities rather than relying only on old research.
4. As an investor allocating new cash, I want deep research limited to at most one or two candidates that could change the decision, so that the review stays focused and stops when no candidate merits further work.
5. As an investor allocating new cash, I want serious candidates compared with a broad-market ETF, cash, and doing nothing, so that a stock is not treated as the default choice.
6. As an investor reviewing a portfolio, I want to include every account, holding, and cash balance from a dated manual or simple CSV snapshot, so that concentration and allocation reflect my whole portfolio.
7. As an investor reviewing my portfolio, I want holdings and quotes refreshed and labeled as of their actual dates, so that current exposures are not based on stale marks presented as live.
8. As an investor reviewing a portfolio, I want current exposures and approximate weights even when I have not supplied a target mix, so that I can understand what I own without the analyst inventing rebalance targets.
9. As an investor who has supplied a baseline, I want proposed rebalancing compared with that baseline, so that target-relative changes use my stated preferences.
10. As an investor reviewing holdings, I want material thesis changes re-underwritten against current evidence, so that prior ownership or prior research does not protect a weak thesis.
11. As an investor reviewing a falling stock, I want the price decline treated as context rather than proof that the thesis failed or that I should buy more, so that the updated case drives the decision.
12. As an investor reviewing concentration, I want direct single-company exposure shown against my configured cap across all accounts, so that conviction cannot silently override my limit.
13. As an investor holding diversified ETFs, I want available sponsor holdings used to show indirect company overlap and its date, so that the analyst can account for known overlap when discussing fit and sizing.
14. As an investor whose ETF look-through data is incomplete, I want overlap marked partial or unknown rather than zero, so that missing holdings are not mistaken for no exposure.
15. As an investor setting exposure limits, I want to choose whether known indirect exposure counts toward a hard cap, so that the analyst does not silently change how my cap works.
16. As an investor with direct exposure already above my cap, I want the excess explained with a forward reduction path, so that an old position is not presented as an approved exception.
17. As an investor setting an active-risk budget, I want individual stocks, sector or theme ETFs, and deliberate macro tilts such as excess cash included, so that active bets share one explicit budget.
18. As an investor whose cap, active budget, baseline, or risk limits are unset, I want those values left unknown until I supply them, so that recommendations do not rely on invented personal guardrails.
19. As an investor evaluating a stock, I want the listing and current price checked and labeled with source and capture time, so that I can distinguish a current quote from a delayed, cached, or user-entered mark.
20. As an investor evaluating a stock, I want its business case compared with relevant holdings, diversified ETFs, cash, and valid actions such as adding, holding, reducing, exiting, or doing nothing, so that company quality is judged in portfolio context.
21. As an investor exploring a theme, I want the analyst to clarify the economic mechanism and create a bounded, agreed shortlist, so that research tests the theme without becoming an unbounded market crawl.
22. As an investor exploring a theme, I want candidates compared with suitable ETF and cash alternatives and no forced buy, so that the theme can reasonably lead to no action.
23. As an investor comparing serious options, I want downside, base, and upside cases over five years, so that I can compare tradeoffs without treating five years as a sell date or natural holding period.
24. As an investor reviewing a company, I want cases built from operating drivers and exit-value sensitivity, so that the analysis does not hide business assumptions inside a single return forecast.
25. As an investor reviewing an ETF, I want cases based on portfolio exposure, fund costs, income, and currency, so that a company valuation method is not misapplied to a fund.
26. As an investor considering cash or short government bills, I want explicit rate and reinvestment paths, so that today's short-term yield is not assumed to last for five years.
27. As an investor comparing cases, I want pivotal assumptions and tradeoffs identified for my review, with judgments such as growth, margins, cash conversion, reinvestment, dilution, FX, discount rate, and exit valuation named separately from calculations, so that I can inspect what drives the result.
28. As an investor, I want scenario cases described as conditional outcomes without invented probabilities or a default weighted value, so that uncertainty is not disguised as precision.
29. As an investor comparing securities in different currencies, I want a reporting currency, local currency, dated FX rate, and known costs shown consistently, so that comparisons use a common basis.
30. As an investor comparing investment outcomes, I want splits and dividends accounted for once with the calculation basis disclosed, so that returns are not double counted.
31. As an investor seeking long-term real-wealth growth, I want the 20+ year objective kept separate from the five-year comparison cases, so that an aspiration is not presented as a return forecast or required hurdle.
32. As an investor, I want facts, calculations, and analyst judgments distinguished, with evidence links and publication or as-of dates, so that I can verify material claims.
33. As an investor reviewing a US issuer, I want material claims checked against issuer material and SEC filings, so that research can be traced to primary evidence.
34. As an investor reviewing a Canadian issuer, I want permitted issuer material and the exact SEDAR+ filing link for my own verification, so that the product does not imply automated SEDAR+ scraping or reuse rights.
35. As an investor, I want missing facts to remain unknown and derived metrics to disclose their source dates and definitions, so that gaps are not mistaken for zero values or reported facts.
36. As an investor with an account such as a TFSA, I want the account type treated as context and tax effects shown only when known, so that the analyst does not presume contribution room or personal tax consequences.
37. As an investor, I want an approximate allocation amount only when the dated snapshot, verified identity, usable quote and FX, applicable cap and budget, and minimal risk context support it, so that missing guardrails produce conditional direction instead of false sizing confidence.
38. As an investor, I want to save the question, as-of date, evidence, conclusion, and any action I later confirm, so that I can revisit the reasoning without the product claiming an unconfirmed trade.
39. As an investor, I want the final answer to state the preferred action, one or two plausible alternatives, what could change the view, and the relevant downside, so that I can make the decision with the tradeoffs in front of me.
40. As an investor, I want the product to leave order placement and execution to me, so that analysis never becomes an automatic trade.

## Implementation Decisions

- Build from an empty repository with a simple standalone full-stack implementation appropriate for a personal application: Next.js frontend → Python/FastAPI backend → OpenAI API reasoning/tool orchestration → financial/research tools → deterministic Python calculations → structured response → frontend. Write new input, display, backend request handling, source-reading, and calculation code. Do not reuse or preserve the old React/Vite frontend, Python request handling, persistence, helpers, tests, or architecture. Keep orchestration in the backend without speculative services or additional orchestration infrastructure. Codex is used only to build the application; it is not the application's runtime.
- Use one decision process for the four request types: identify the request, establish dated holdings and cash plus minimal relevant context, review relevant evidence and alternatives, apply portfolio guardrails, then return the recommendation contract. New-cash requests always include a lightweight fresh scan. Theme exploration uses a separately bounded, user-agreed shortlist. Other reviews stay focused on the named stock, current holdings, and relevant alternatives unless another candidate could change the decision.
- Accept manual entry or simple CSV holdings. Allocation analysis needs an as-of date, security and listing identity, shares or cash, quote currency, current quote or labeled user mark, and reporting currency. Use cost basis only for relevant tax or transaction consequences; it must not anchor the current thesis or today's recommendation. Account for splits and dividends once, disclose the calculation basis, and never double count adjusted prices and dividends.
- Keep holdings values, weights, concentration, direct cap, active budget, available ETF overlap, screens, ratios, and scenario arithmetic deterministic and inspectable. Use small new Python helpers in the backend and ordinary table and formula tools where suitable; do not add a library solely for its own sake. The data tools do not validate finance definitions or upstream evidence.
- Use AI for bounded research, source-backed evidence synthesis, thesis challenge, scenario-driver judgments, comparative ranking, and explanation. AI may propose a justified exposure range; deterministic calculations translate it into amounts and enforce portfolio guardrails. There is no claimed formula for objectively optimal sizing, and conviction cannot waive a configured cap or budget. Reverse valuation describes the performance required under named assumptions, not a unique claim about what the market believes.
- Use pandas and NumPy for tables, screening, ratios, portfolio arithmetic, and explicit scenario formulas in the new backend Python helpers. Add numpy-financial only if its periodic cash-flow functions simplify a required calculation. SEC EDGAR is the primary US filing source; edgartools is an optional convenience parser, with the original filing as authority. Use yfinance only as the qualified indicative-price candidate described in the handoff, and Bank of Canada Valet for suitable dated CAD FX. Do not introduce OpenBB, FinanceToolkit, optimizers, or broad analytics packages as mandatory layers.
- Preserve financial periods, units, currencies, and metric definitions. Check company facts against filings where custom tags, segments, or notes matter. Use sector-appropriate ratios and mid-cycle context for cyclicals. Earnings releases, prepared remarks, and available webcasts can supply evidence; never imply that unavailable transcript Q&A was reviewed.
- Use issuer investor-relations material and verify material US company claims against SEC EDGAR filings. For Canadian issuers, use permitted issuer material and provide a user-opened SEDAR+ verification link; automated SEDAR+ scraping or database construction is not part of the product. For thesis-specific macro claims, use the relevant agency series only when it tests a named mechanism.
- Use dated full sponsor holdings for ETF look-through where available, including underlying funds where possible. Label incomplete look-through as partial or unknown. Do not use a top-ten list or periodic SEC N-PORT data as if it were a complete current holdings file.
- For indicative prices, a qualified personal-use source may be used only after coverage and terms are checked. A user-entered broker-display mark is the fallback. Record source and capture time, show quote age, and never label stale, cached, or manual data as live. Use an appropriate dated FX source for the reporting currency; a rate is indicative, not an execution quote.
- Compare serious options in the same reporting currency and on the same as-of basis. Include known costs and tax effects where available, and leave unknown costs or tax consequences unquantified. Treat FX paths as assumptions; do not label scenarios as real purchasing-power outcomes unless inflation is sourced and modeled.
- Store only the dated question, evidence references, reasoning, conclusion, and an optional user-confirmed action needed for later review. Implement storage from scratch using plain local files. Do not require a database or performance-tracking engine.
- The FastAPI backend owns the OpenAI integration, reasoning/tool orchestration, and secrets. The frontend submits questions and portfolio context to the backend and displays its structured response. Supply API keys through server-side configuration; the frontend never receives them, and they must not appear in frontend assets or response payloads. The backend invokes financial/research tools and deterministic Python calculations, applies portfolio guardrails, and validates the structured recommendation before returning it. All four workflows extend the same backend decision pipeline. The deployed application runs independently of Codex and does not launch a Codex CLI or app-server runtime.

## Testing Decisions

- Prefer one highest-level product-facing decision journey over separate tests for each internal component. Create a small new test suite around the application request/result boundary: frontend input → FastAPI request handling and shared decision pipeline → structured response → displayed recommendation. Exercise all four complete question-to-recommendation workflows with controlled inputs and fake model/data providers, checking externally visible behavior rather than internal implementation details.
- Use controlled dated portfolio snapshots, evidence and quote fixtures, and fake OpenAI/model and financial/research-data providers. Script fake model responses and tool requests so automated tests exercise backend orchestration, deterministic calculations, guardrails, and structured answers without live OpenAI or market-data calls. Fixed answers test rendering and the answer contract, not the quality of AI research or judgment; assess that separately by reviewing completed application answers against the same scenarios.
- Assert the external answer contract: preferred action; amount only when justified; alternatives; five-year conditional cases for serious options; named assumptions and uncertainty; source links and dates; and clear treatment of stale, missing, partial, or user-entered data.
- Include behavior checks for post-allocation direct-cap and active-budget weights, incomplete ETF overlap, no invented rebalance target when there is no baseline, unresolved identity or stale quote/FX producing conditional direction, and contradictory or absent facts remaining unknown.
- Verify that a new-cash request performs the bounded fresh scan, that deep research stops at no more than one or two decision-changing candidates, and that a theme request stays within its agreed shortlist and effort bound.
- Verify that the response never invents a trade, probability, confident allocation amount without required inputs, or a claim that an unconfirmed action occurred.
- Verify at the application boundary that server-side API keys are absent from frontend assets and response payloads and that model/tool outputs cannot bypass deterministic portfolio guardrails.

## Out of Scope

- Automatic orders, broker execution, or claims that a recommendation was traded.
- Background monitoring, alerts, continuous portfolio refresh, or automatic research runs.
- Full transaction history, performance attribution, CAGR/XIRR from a single snapshot, or a portfolio performance engine.
- Portfolio optimization, backtesting, Monte Carlo or probability engines, and weighted expected value by default.
- Complex tax calculations, presumed TFSA contribution room, or automatic personal tax advice.
- Unbounded market discovery, a broad always-on candidate crawl, or deep research on every holding and screen result.
- Automated SEDAR+ scraping, broad transcript ingestion, and new data subscriptions unless an agreed requirement justifies their coverage and reuse terms.
- Multi-user access, authentication redesign, institution governance, queues, workflow engines, additional infrastructure beyond the standalone frontend/backend, or a new mandatory database.
- Hardcoded numeric direct-company caps, active budgets, default asset mixes, or personal risk limits; these remain unset until the user supplies them.

## Further Notes

The long-term objective is real-wealth accumulation over 20+ years with no known withdrawal deadline. The prior 18–20% aspiration is neither a required return nor a forecast. Five-year scenarios compare current choices; they do not set an exit date. A missing ordinary mix limits target-relative rebalancing but does not prevent current exposure review.

Current holdings and concentration can be reviewed without a target baseline. A confident allocation amount requires a dated snapshot, verified security identity, usable quote and FX, applicable numeric company cap and active budget, and minimal risk/context inputs. If a decisive fact is missing, state what is unresolved and give conditional direction or no allocation recommendation. Incomplete ETF look-through qualifies overlap but does not by itself block all numeric sizing.

The deployed application is independent of the Codex development session. Codex builds the application; the Next.js frontend and Python/FastAPI backend provide its runtime, with backend-owned OpenAI API reasoning and tool orchestration. The frontend can initiate analysis through the backend without access to OpenAI API keys. This architecture replaces the previous native-Codex-runtime assumption and preserves the product's single shared decision pipeline and financial-analysis scope.

The product's source and data terms vary by issuer and provider. Fresh coverage and reuse research is not part of this specification. Prices, holdings, filings, and forecasts can be stale or incomplete; calculated outputs may be repeatable while assumptions remain uncertain. Keep evidence dates, source limitations, and uncertainty visible.

The testing boundary is the application request-to-result journey with fake model and data providers. The approved implementation tickets are local Markdown files marked `ready-for-agent`; their numbered priority and direct dependencies remain as approved. This architecture correction does not add product scope.
